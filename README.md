# Dark matter halo mass functions and density profiles from mass and energy cascade

Xu Z., 2026, *Dark matter halo mass functions and density profiles from mass and energy cascade*, Zenodo [doi:10.5281/zenodo.24095289](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24095289). The earlier version is hosted on arXiv as [arXiv:2210.01200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01200), and the published article is *Scientific Reports* **13**, 16531 (2023), [doi:10.1038/s41598-023-42958-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-42958-6).

## Major findings

- **Halo mass function.** Haloes grow by merging, and a halo of mass m<sub>h</sub> waits a time τ<sub>g</sub> ∝ m<sub>h</sub><sup>−λ</sup> before its next merger. The Fokker–Planck solution of this random walk gives a power law at small mass and an exponential cut-off at large mass. With two values of λ (below and above a critical mass m<sub>h</sub>*) it fits the Illustris-1-Dark simulation better than the Press–Schechter, Sheth–Tormen, Jenkins, Warren and Tinker functions: rms of ln f 0.14 against 0.27–0.46. Press–Schechter is the special case λ = 2/3.
- **Density profile.** A particle random walk with waiting time τ<sub>g</sub> ∝ r<sup>−γ</sup> gives a double-γ density profile; Einasto's profile is a special case. With γ = 2/3 the inner slope is −4/3, the slope predicted by the energy cascade. Fits with the inner slope left free give about −1.3 at z = 4 and for massive haloes at z = 0.
- **Small-scale permanence.** Near the centre, the profiles of critical-mass haloes at all redshifts approach the same r<sup>−4/3</sup> law. The slope steepens from −1.1 at z = 12 to −1.4 at z = 0, and the amplitude at 5 kpc stays within a factor of two.
- **Measured exponent.** The mass-cascade exponent is λ = 0.94 (slopes 0.92–0.97 for z ≤ 2 below 0.1 m<sub>h</sub>*).
- **Shape parameter.** The shape parameter β<sub>r</sub> lies between 0.84 and 3.44. It decreases with halo mass at z ≥ 2 and is nearly constant (0.84–1.43) at z = 0.
